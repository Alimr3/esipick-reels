What does one F-35 flight hour really cost? The Pentagon's FY2026 rate sheet bills an F-35A at $18,600 an hour — but GAO and CBO figures point to a full cost closer to $47,000 (our estimate). We open the books: where the money goes, why the F-35 gets pricier per hour the less it flies, and what a $1.6 trillion sustainment bill means for U.S. taxpayers.

Key numbers
• $18,600/hr — F-35A "Other DoD" reimbursable rate, FY2026 (F-16C $14,818 · F-15EX $17,321 · F-15E $29,992 · F-22A $55,051 · B-52H $66,003)
• $6.6M per F-35A per year — Air Force projected operating & sustainment cost (constant 2012 $) (GAO, Apr 2024)
• 195 hours — average F-35A flight hours per jet in 2024, vs a 250-hour benchmark (CBO, Jun 2025)
• ≈$47,000/hr — Apex estimate: $6.6M ÷ 195 h ≈ $33,800 (2012 $), adjusted for inflation. At 250 h the same math gives ≈$37,000.
• 67% → 44% — fleet mission capable rate, FY2021 → FY2025; fully mission capable 38% → 25% (GAO, Jun 2026)
• $1.58 trillion — lifetime sustainment estimate, up 44% from $1.1T in 2018; jets planned to fly to 2088 (GAO, Apr 2024)

Caveats
• The reimbursable rate is what one DoD component bills another for flying hours; it is not the full cost of owning the aircraft.
• Our ≈$47,000 and ≈$37,000 per-hour figures are Apex estimates derived from GAO and CBO numbers (projected annual cost ÷ actual hours, CPI-adjusted). Official cost-per-hour definitions vary by source and year.
• GAO dollar figures are in constant 2012 dollars unless noted.
