Pentagon drone cost: the Drone Dominance program promised small attack drones at $2,300 each, about 13,000 for the price of one MQ-9 Reaper. At the prices the Pentagon itself posted in 2026, an armed long-range drone comes to about $7,750, so one Reaper buys about 3,900. Here's the full math, why U.S.-made drones cost more, and the other half of the bill: stopping the other side's drones.

Key numbers
• Drone Dominance: ~$1B over two years; 4 phases, 30K → 60K → 100K → 150K drones at $5,000 → $3,000 → $2,300 (DoD RFI, Dec. 2025). Site now: "over 200,000" by 2027.
• Gauntlet I (Feb. 2026): 11 winners; ~30,000 drones for ~$150M, inert payload (DefenseScoop)
• Gauntlet II posted prices: $4,500 long-range drone, $3,500 close-quarters, $3,250 munition (RFS; GovCon IC)
• ~$30 million: MQ-9 Reaper unit cost (CBO estimate)
• DJI accounts for an estimated 70–90% of drones used in the U.S. (Special Competitive Studies Project)
• Non-Chinese parts cost 25–70% more; break-even $2,000–3,000; Thunder Tiger delivered 1,520 drones for ~$7.6M (Taiwanese makers via Army Recognition / CNA)
• Shahed-136 $30K–$50K; Merops interceptor ~$15K, 13,000 bought in ~8 days (Army Sec. Driscoll via The War Zone)
• PAC-3 MSE ~$3.9M (FY2026 budget via CSIS) · up to 25,000 APKWS kits/yr, Pentagon–BAE framework Oct. 19, 2026 (Defense News)
• FY2027 request: $53.6B incl. $16.9B unmanned buys, $14.4B counter-drone; Hegseth's ~$50B one-time ask not approved (Defense News)

Our math (Apex Reports)
• $4,500 + $3,250 = $7,750 for an armed long-range drone at posted Gauntlet II prices (more than 3x the $2,300 goal)
• $30M ÷ $2,300 ≈ 13,000 drones; $30M ÷ $7,750 ≈ 3,900 (rounded)
• $3.9M ÷ $30K–$50K ≈ 80–130x · $1.74B ÷ 55,000 kits (Navy, 2025) ≈ $32,000/kit (rough)

Caveats: a Reaper and a small attack drone do different jobs (price comparison, not capability). Posted prices are ceilings, not final totals; per-drone figures from order totals are averages.
