Gas prices in a Gulf war: Brent crude touched $110 a barrel on Oct. 17, 2026, days after a missile got through to Saudi Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran. U.S. regular gas now averages $4.30 a gallon (AAA), up from about $2.91 in February. So what does a Gulf war really do to your gas tank? We put 1973, 1990 and 2026 side by side.

Key numbers
• Brent: ≈$110 high on Oct. 17, 2026 (closed ≈$109.7); pre-war close ≈$73 (Feb. 27) → up more than 50%
• U.S. regular gas: $4.30 (AAA, Oct. 17) vs $2.91 (EIA Feb. 2026 average) → up ≈48%
• Hormuz: ≈20 million barrels/day in 2024 ≈ one-fifth of world oil use, >25% of seaborne oil trade; bypass pipelines ≈2.6M b/d spare (EIA estimate)
• 1973–74: oil ≈$3 → nearly $12 a barrel; U.S. gas 38.5¢ (May 1973) → 55.1¢ (June 1974), +43%
• 1990: crude ≈$17 (July avg) → ≈$36 (Oct. avg); U.S. regular unleaded $1.08 → $1.38, +27% (BLS)
• Jan. 1991: first emergency SPR drawdown, ≈17 million barrels sold (DOE)
• U.S. a net petroleum exporter since 2020 (first time since at least 1949, EIA); ≈0.5M b/d of crude from the Persian Gulf in 2024 ≈ 2% of U.S. petroleum use
• SPR: 172M-barrel release announced March 2026 (part of a 400M-barrel IEA action); ≈283M barrels on Oct. 15, 2026 = 39.6% of 714M capacity

Caveats
• Our rule of thumb (42 gallons per barrel → +$10/barrel ≈ +24¢/gallon) covers crude cost only, before refining, distribution and taxes. Apex math, not an official figure.
• Historical oil prices are rounded monthly/annual averages; sources differ slightly. Nothing here is a price forecast or financial advice.
• Aramco Dhahran (Oct. 13): Saudi Press Agency described a limited fire with no casualties; attribution per Saudi-led coalition and media reports.
