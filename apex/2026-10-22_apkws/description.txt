A $3.9 million Patriot interceptor to stop a drone that costs $30,000 to $50,000. On Oct. 19, 2026, the Pentagon signed a framework with BAE Systems for up to 25,000 APKWS laser-guidance kits a year, a far cheaper way to shoot down drones. Here's the math.

Key numbers
• Up to 25,000 APKWS kits a year, for as long as seven years, under a Pentagon–BAE Systems framework signed Oct. 19, 2026; Congress still has to approve and fund the buys (Defense News; Defense Daily; Inside Defense)
• APKWS turns an unguided 70 mm (2.75-inch) rocket into a laser-guided munition; the Pentagon calls it a cheaper alternative to air-to-air missiles against drones (Defense News)
• Up to $1.74 billion for up to 55,000 kits: U.S. Navy contract, August 2025 (BAE Systems)
• ~$3.9 million: one PAC-3 MSE Patriot interceptor (FY2026 budget figures, as cited by CSIS)
• $30,000–$50,000: one Iranian Shahed-136 drone, per Army Secretary Daniel Driscoll (The War Zone, April 2026)

Our math (Apex Reports, rough)
• $1.74B ÷ 55,000 kits ≈ $32,000 per kit (contract ceiling; not an official unit price)
• $3.9M ÷ ~$32,000 ≈ 120 kits for the price of one Patriot
• $3.9M ÷ $50,000 ≈ 78x the drone's high-end price

Caveats: 25,000 a year is a ceiling, not a firm order, and the framework's value wasn't announced. A kit needs a rocket, a launcher and a laser designator; it is not a like-for-like replacement for Patriot, which also defends against ballistic missiles.
