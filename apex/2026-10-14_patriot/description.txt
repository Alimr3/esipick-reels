Patriot missile shortage: the U.S. may have fewer than 1,000 top-tier PAC-3 interceptors left after the Iran war — maybe as few as ~760, by CSIS's estimate.
Lockheed builds about 600 a year, but America has bought an average of just 183 a year. We run the numbers on how long it takes to refill the shield, what it costs, and why 2030 is the date that matters.

KEY NUMBERS
• ~30 Patriot interceptors fired in one night defending Muwaffaq Salti Air Base, Jordan, in September (U.S. official via Reuters)
• PAC-3 unit cost: ~$3.9M (FY2026 budget documents via CSIS)
• Pre-war PAC-3 stockpile: ~2,330 (CSIS estimate); 1,060–1,430 used by the April 8 ceasefire; ~65% used by late July, leaving fewer than 1,000 — as few as 759 (CSIS)
• Lockheed Martin delivered 620 PAC-3 MSE in 2025 (all customers); the U.S. 5-year average purchase is 183/yr; about half of production goes to 18 other countries (CSIS)
• Lead time from contract to delivery: 42 months (CSIS)
• Plan: ~2,000 PAC-3 MSE a year under a seven-year framework (Lockheed / Pentagon, Jan. 2026); CSIS says planned by 2030; Army contract worth up to $58.6B through FY2032
• THAAD: ~$15.5M each, ~360 pre-war, roughly half used by April; no deliveries since Aug. 2023, expected to resume by April 2027 (CSIS)
• SM-3: ~$28.7M each, ~410 pre-war, 130–250 used, ~53/yr. SM-6: ~$5.3M, ~1,160 pre-war, 190–370 used (CSIS)

APEX MATH (our own arithmetic, labelled in the video)
• 30 × $3.9M ≈ $117M for one night
• 2,330 − ~760 ≈ 1,570 PAC-3s to replace ≈ $6.1B at $3.9M each
• Refill time: ~8.6 years at 183/yr · ~5.2 years at ~300/yr (half of 2025 output) · ~1.6 years at ~1,000/yr (half of 2,000) once the line reaches full rate
• An interceptor ordered in Oct. 2026 + 42 months ≈ April 2030
• ~760 ÷ 30 per night ≈ 25 nights like Sept. 8 (illustrative only)

CAVEATS
• Interceptor inventories are classified. All stockpile figures are CSIS estimates built from public budget documents; the Pentagon has not confirmed them and says the U.S. "has everything it needs to execute."
• Production numbers include foreign sales; the U.S. share varies year to year.
