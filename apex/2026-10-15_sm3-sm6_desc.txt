The Navy's two key ship-launched interceptors are in very different shape after the Iran war. The SM-3 costs about $28.7M, may have lost up to 61% of its pre-war stock, and takes 4–5 years from order to delivery. The SM-6 is cheaper, deeper and about to be built much faster.

Key numbers (CSIS "Last Rounds?", Apr 2026, unless noted)
• SM-3: ~$28.7M each (CBO: ~$28M) · ~410 before the war · 130–250 used · ~53 built a year (Block IB 40 + Block IIA 13, five-year average) · 52–64 months from contract to full delivery
• SM-6: ~$5.3M each (CBO: ~$4M) · ~1,160 before the war · 190–370 used · ~96 a year (five-year average) · plan: more than 500 a year
• Oct 2026: Navy awarded Raytheon an SM-6 contract worth up to $24.4B (5 years + options) and the Missile Defense Agency a $6.3B SM-3 Block IB contract

Our math (Apex Reports, estimates)
• SM-3 used ÷ 53 a year ≈ 2.5–4.7 years of production to replace
• 130–250 of ~410 ≈ 32–61% of the SM-3 stock; 190–370 of ~1,160 ≈ 16–32% of the SM-6 stock
• One SM-3 ≈ the price of 5 SM-6s ($28.7M ÷ $5.3M)
• An SM-3 ordered in Oct 2026 + 52–64 months ≈ delivered 2031–2032

Caveats: Interceptor inventories are classified. Stockpile and usage figures are independent estimates (CSIS); the Pentagon has not confirmed them. Other estimates differ (e.g. The War Zone reports ~400 SM-3s and ~1,250 SM-6s pre-war). "Used" means fired, not lost.
