The MQ-9 Reaper fleet fell from 351 to about 135 — and the Iran war isn't the main reason. We break down every public Reaper loss count, the real bill, and why the U.S. can't simply build more.

The Air Force reported 351 MQ-9A Reapers in FY2022. By May 2026, about 135 were left. 186 were gone before the war with Iran even started (retired Block 1 aircraft, plus accidents and earlier losses). Now the war is burning through the rest, the production line is closed, and the $10 million replacement doesn't exist yet.

KEY NUMBERS
• 351 MQ-9As in FY2022 → 165 at the start of FY2026 → ~135 in May 2026 (CRS; USAF via TWZ)
• Reaper losses in the Iran war, by source: DoD Lead IG "as many as 30" (through Jun 30) · CBO 24 (through Aug 1) · U.S. officials to The Washington Post "at least 45" (Aug 13, incl. Yemen) · The War Zone 47 (through Sep 18). The Air Force has not disclosed an official number.
• Unit cost: ~$30M (CBO) to "up to $50M" with full sensors (USAF testimony). 45 lost ≈ $1.35B–$2.25B (Apex estimate)
• Yemen, spring 2025: 7 Reapers down in under six weeks, >$200M (U.S. officials via AP)
• 56 combat lines; 135 ÷ 56 ≈ 2.4 aircraft per line (Apex math)
• Production line closed; 2–3 years to restart (General Atomics via CRS). FY2027 request: $766.2M for the MQ-9 program, zero new Air Force Reapers (5 for the Marine Corps). Buyback: fewer than 10 unused airframes.
• Successor — Massed Modular Aircraft: ~$10M air vehicle (no sensors), up to 4 prototype firms by end-2026, procurement request planned FY2028; fleet goal reported as 500 by 2032 (CRS) vs "at least 180" (Maj. Gen. Niemi). CCA drone wingman ≈ $20M.

CAVEATS
• "Lost" includes aircraft shot down, crashed after link failures and destroyed on the ground; counts cover different dates and theaters, so they are not directly comparable.
• Dollar totals marked "Apex estimate/Apex math" are our own arithmetic from the cited unit costs, not official figures.
• MMA and CCA prices are for the air vehicle only and aren't a like-for-like comparison with a fully equipped Reaper.
