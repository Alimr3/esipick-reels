China doesn't publish how many J-20 stealth fighters it has. Western estimates range from about 300 in service to around 500 delivered, and the UK's RUSI estimates production at roughly 120 a year. Here is what that pace means next to America's F-22 and F-35 numbers.

Key numbers
• ~300 J-20s in service across at least 13 PLA Air Force regiments: RUSI (late 2025)
• ~500 J-20s delivered by mid-2026: analyst Andreas Rupprecht, via The War Zone (Jul 2026)
• ~120 J-20s built per year: RUSI estimate (late 2025)
• ~1,000 J-20s of all versions by 2030: RUSI projection
• ~185 F-22 Raptors in the U.S. Air Force (143 combat-coded), out of production
• FY2027 request: 85 F-35s, but only 32 in the base budget; the other 53 depend on a $350B reconciliation request Congress has not passed
• Nearly 1,300 F-35s operational worldwide with the U.S. and allies (Lockheed Martin, Jan 2026); record 191 delivered in 2025
• Our math (estimate): 185 F-22s ÷ ~120 J-20s a year ≈ 1.5 years, so China adds an F-22-sized fleet about every 18 months at RUSI's rate

Caveats: J-20 numbers are open-source estimates; "in service" and "delivered" are not the same count and come from different dates. Fleet size is not combat power: training, sensors, weapons, maintenance and allies all matter. The 2030 figure is a projection, not a fact. F-35 totals include allied jets.

Does fleet size matter more than quality — or is the U.S. betting on the wrong side of that question? 👇
